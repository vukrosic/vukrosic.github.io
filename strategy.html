Lane 01 · Databases

The fastest path to a credible commercial proof.

Start with teams that own a costly data-system hot path and can deploy a patch. Open code and deterministic semantics let us prove a narrow claim before asking a customer to trust the system.

Algorithms to search

  • Hash and grouped aggregation
  • Hash, merge, and nested-loop joins
  • Join ordering and cardinality estimation
  • Sorting, Top-N, partitioning, and spilling
  • Scans, filters, and vectorized expressions
  • Indexes, range lookup, caching, and prefetch
  • Compression, encoding, and late materialization
  • Shuffle, skew, ingestion, and compaction

Who buys

  • Database and analytical-engine vendors
  • Warehouses, lakehouses, and query platforms
  • ETL, observability, and data-infrastructure companies
  • Teams operating a costly internal data platform

Economic proof: translate the speedup into lower cloud spend, more workload per machine, lower tail latency, or delayed infrastructure expansion.

How to run the database experiment

01 · PIN

Pin engine commit, compiler, flags, machine, and the untouched baseline binary.

02 · FREEZE

Freeze exact-result and schema checks, customer-like query shapes, visible cases, and hidden confirmation cases.

03 · PROFILE

Profile first. Give each search lane a distinct mechanism and isolated candidate folder.

04 · VERIFY

Build one candidate at a time. Run semantic and adversarial SQL checks before timing.

05 · MEASURE

Interleave baseline and candidate trials. Report medians, tails, variability, and peak resident memory.

06 · CONFIRM

Rebuild a provisional winner cleanly and confirm it on untouched cases without tuning.

Example pilot gate

Freeze the real threshold before search.

Correctness
Zero output, schema, semantic, or exception mismatches.
Primary objective
A customer-agreed suite threshold; for example, at least 1.05× geometric-mean speedup.
Regression bound
No critical workload below the predeclared floor.
Memory
Peak memory remains within the agreed cap.
Confirmation
Fresh clean build, held-out workloads, and a low-contention rerun.
Claim
Report only the workload, hardware, and metric the evidence directly supports.
Lane 02 · Logistics

Potentially larger wins. Harder reality.

Logistics becomes attractive only with a design partner that supplies real replay data, the complete constraint system, and the business objective used in production.

Algorithms to search

  • Capacitated vehicle routing
  • Time windows, breaks, skills, and service rules
  • Dynamic dispatch and same-day insertion
  • Pickup-and-delivery matching
  • Fleet assignment and empty-mile reduction
  • Warehouse picking, slotting, and wave planning
  • Load planning, packing, and utilization
  • Workforce, dock, and delivery scheduling

Who buys

  • Route-optimization and field-service platforms
  • 3PL, freight, delivery, and fleet technology companies
  • Fulfillment, warehouse, and marketplace operators
  • Large operators that control dispatch and planning

Required inputs: historical orders, resources, travel times, every hard constraint, the real cost function, and the production planner used today.

How to run the logistics experiment

01 · REPLAY

Obtain anonymized historical jobs or a realistic simulator plus the complete hard-constraint validator.

02 · OBJECTIVE

Record the production baseline and weighted cost: distance, time, labor, vehicles, lateness, cancellations, and service.

03 · HOLDOUT

Split replay chronologically. Keep seasonal, high-stress, and untouched confirmation periods.

04 · SEARCH

Search heuristics, neighborhoods, initialization, decomposition, batching, and policies only on development periods.

05 · STRESS

Require every hard constraint to pass, then compare cost and service distributions across seeds and stress scenarios.

06 · SHADOW

Run in shadow mode before live decisions. Operators retain approval, rollback, and incident controls.

Logistics decision gate

Cheaper is valid only when the plan remains feasible.

Hard constraints
Every capacity, time-window, skill, break, compatibility, and policy rule passes.
Economic objective
Improvement on the actual weighted business cost, not only a synthetic distance proxy.
Service floor
No unacceptable deterioration in lateness, cancellations, completion, or operator workload.
Robustness
The gain persists across seeds, peak days, disruptions, and untouched chronological holdouts.
Deployment safety
Shadow mode, human approval, rollback, and explicit incident thresholds.
Claim
No causal or live savings claim until a controlled production rollout measures it.
The universal experiment contract

Search aggressively.
Measure conservatively.

The domain changes. The evidence rules do not. A candidate earns promotion only after it passes the contract that existed before the result.

01

Define the economic objective

Name one primary metric and the capability it represents: latency, throughput, memory, cloud cost, route cost, utilization, or service quality.

02

Freeze the contract

Pin source, toolchain, baseline, evaluator, visible cases, held-out cases, constraints, resource limits, and the decision rule before search.

03

Search isolated candidates

Every hypothesis gets a durable folder and patch. Cheap checks run first. Candidates never edit the baseline, evaluator, or one another.

04

Prove correctness first

Compare outputs and semantics, then exercise edge, randomized, adversarial, concurrency, failure, and unsupported-path behavior before timing.

05

Measure under control

Use the same hardware and toolchain, warmups, repeated trials, interleaved order, median and tail statistics, peak memory, and quiet timing windows.

06

Confirm or reject

Rebuild the winner from the pinned baseline and rerun held-out cases. Preserve regressions and negative results. Promote only what the rule supports.

Resource & agent coordination

Parallel ideas.
Serialized evidence.

  • ✓

    One owner per candidate and one durable write location.

  • ✓

    Scout mechanisms broadly in parallel; serialize compilers and claim-bearing benchmarks.

  • ✓

    Never time a candidate while another build or high-CPU scan competes for the machine.

  • ✓

    Record CPU, memory, swap, toolchain, source hash, build parallelism, and deviations.

  • ✓

    Run cheap correctness and targeted screens before long confirmation suites.

  • ✓

    Keep failed builds, slower candidates, noisy runs, and blocked paths as evidence.

Open Discovery claims only what the frozen evaluator, representative workload, resource evidence, and independent confirmation support.
